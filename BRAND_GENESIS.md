# TOHSENO Brand Genesis

**Version:** 0.2.0
**Status:** Canonical, brand constitution
**Scope:** protocol, CLI, Studio, website, documentation, launch material, provenance, and any official TOHSENO surface.

This file belongs at the root of the monorepo.

When implementation and this document disagree, the disagreement is a design decision to resolve. It is not permission to ignore the design.

---

## 1. The declaration

Software used to be made by other people for us. TOHSENO moves software toward something a person can make for their own life.

TOHSENO exists to make this sentence ordinary:

> I noticed something my life needed, took a Shot, ran it on my iPhone, learned from using it, and evolved it.

The old unit was the finished app. The new unit is the **Shot**: one coherent intention carried into contact with reality as working software.

One person is enough. Private is valid. Strange is useful. An app does not need a market, a company, a roadmap, or an audience to deserve to exist.

## 2. The Apple boundary

TOHSENO is specifically for the Apple ecosystem.

At launch, the product claim is precise:

> **TOHSENO turns one coherent intention into a native iPhone app you own, use, and evolve.**

The larger horizon is native personal software across Apple platforms. The shipped promise begins with iPhone.

Brand implications:

- Never describe TOHSENO as cross-platform.
- Never use Android devices, Android UI, or generic phone mockups in official material.
- Use **native iPhone app** in conversion copy until another Apple platform is genuinely supported.
- Use **personal software for the Apple ecosystem** in movement-level copy.
- When technically true, say Swift and SwiftUI plainly. Do not use native technology as decorative jargon.
- Generated apps should feel at home on Apple devices, but they must not all look like TOHSENO.

The Apple focus is not a badge. It is a design boundary. Focus creates coherence.

## 4. Character

TOHSENO is disciplined, black, quiet, precise, and almost severe.

It is the soup of cosmic potential before an intention takes form. The shell does not perform creativity. It makes room for creativity.

The brand should feel:

- rigorous enough to trust with source code and ownership;
- restrained enough that a user's work becomes the most alive thing on screen;
- direct enough for somebody who has never written code;
- strange enough to suggest a genuinely new relationship with software;
- prolific rather than precious.

The correct tension is **elite in taste, democratic in authorship**.

## 5. The logo family

There are three optical forms and two signatures. No additional decorative variants should be invented.

All circle forms are rendered in **Chemical Amber**. The former golden-yellow recolor is deprecated; it must not resurface from earlier exports. The heated ring is canon: a coal, not a halo.

### 5.1 Genesis Circle

File: `brand/logos/tohseno-genesis-circle.png`

The original textured stroke in Chemical Amber. It is ceremonial and human. Use it on the whitepaper, launch pieces, graduation records, posters, physical objects, and rare moments where TOHSENO speaks as a movement.

The Genesis Circle keeps its brush texture and internal tonal variation. Do not "clean it up" into a flat ring; the texture is the human gesture. Flat rings are what the Core and Micro Circles are for.

Minimum size: **128 px** on screen. Never use it as a favicon or small UI icon.

### 5.2 Core Circle

Files: `brand/logos/tohseno-core-circle.svg` and `.png`

A flat operational distillation of the Genesis Circle. It retains the irregular silhouette without the internal brush texture. Use it in Studio, web navigation, documentation, provenance, and normal brand surfaces.

Minimum size: **32 px**.

The SVG uses `currentColor` and defaults to Chemical Amber. Override it only with Bone or Void.

### 5.3 Micro Circle

Files: `brand/logos/tohseno-micro-circle.svg` and `.png`

An optically simplified mark for **16–32 px**. Use it for favicons, compact controls, status marks, and tiny signatures. Do not shrink the Genesis Circle and hope it survives.

### 5.4 Horizontal Signature

Files: `brand/logos/tohseno-signature-dark.svg` and `tohseno-signature-light.svg`

The circle carries the human gesture. The wordmark stays severe, quiet, and widely spaced. Do not add a tagline inside the Horizontal Signature.

### 5.5 Campaign Signature

Files: `brand/logos/tohseno-campaign-signature-dark.svg` and `tohseno-campaign-signature-light.svg`

The wordmark with **take another shot** set beneath it in Tohseno Mono, lowercase. This is the only lockup permitted to carry the tagline.

Permitted: launch material, movement-level pieces, posters, social, the whitepaper family.
Prohibited: Studio, documentation, provenance records, app icon, favicons, and any product UI.

The Campaign Signature is a flag, not a label. When TOHSENO is working, it is silent; the flag flies only when TOHSENO is speaking.

### 5.6 App icon

File: `brand/logos/tohseno-app-icon-1024.png`

The app icon is a Void field with the Micro Circle. It is not a glossy coin, portal render, glowing object, or gradient.

### 5.7 Logo rules

- Never rotate the fixed TOHSENO mark.
- Never add a glow, chrome, bevel, shadow, gradient, or 3D treatment.
- Never fill the center.
- Never animate it as a generic loading spinner.
- Never put the mark in every generated app. TOHSENO signs provenance; it does not watermark a person's software.
- Keep clear space equal to at least one ring thickness.
- A maker's hand-drawn graduation circle is a protocol artifact, not a new official logo variant.

## 6. The color system

The palette is intentionally small.

| Token          |       Hex | Role                                                 |
| -------------- | --------: | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Void           | `#050505` | Primary field. The space before form.                |
| Carbon         | `#0D0D0E` | Main panels and persistent surfaces.                 |
| Graphite       | `#19191A` | Raised surfaces, selected rows, device shell.        |
| Iron           | `#2B2B2D` | Hairlines, borders, separators.                      |
| Ash            | `#686964` | Disabled states and tertiary information.            |
| Silver         | `#A7A8A2` | Secondary text, metadata, timestamps.                |
| Bone           | `#F1EDE4` | Primary text and editorial light surface.            |
| Chemical Amber | `#FF5A00` | Human agency: commit, select, circle, take the Shot. |
| Ember          | `#351607` | Rare accent wash behind an amber action.             |

No pure white. No default pure black. No rainbow brand palette.

### The law of orange

**Chemical Amber means a human decision.**

It may appear when a person:

- commits an intention;
- takes a Shot;
- deliberately selects an evolution;
- circles something that became real.

It must not be used to make every card, heading, icon, hover, or decorative flourish feel "on brand." Orange becomes powerful through scarcity.

Do not use Chemical Amber as small body text on Bone. On light surfaces, use Void for text and reserve amber for marks, rules, and large actions.

### Rendering amber

Flat surfaces (keylines, indicators, rules, buttons, Core and Micro Circles) use pure `#FF5A00`. The Genesis Circle alone may carry textured, multi-tonal amber, because it alone carries the brush. Nobody may later flatten the Genesis Circle or texture the flat marks; the two treatments are distinct on purpose.

## 7. How the palette behaves in Studio

The Studio contains three visual territories: the Shot list, the intent composer, and the simulator.

### Shot list

The shell is Void and Carbon. Rows are separated by Iron. Titles are Bone; metadata is Silver.

App icons retain their own colors. They are not tinted, desaturated, framed in amber, or forced into a TOHSENO template. The user's body of software should be the most colorful thing in the Studio.

A selected Shot receives a thin amber keyline or small amber indicator. Do not fill the whole row orange.

### Intent composer

The composer is the deepest field: Carbon on Void, with Bone text and Silver supporting copy.

The caret may be amber. The **Take Shot** action becomes amber only when the intention is ready to commit. Before that moment it remains neutral.

The composer should feel less like a chat box and more like a place where an intention is being made exact.

### Simulator

The simulator frame is Graphite, Iron, and Silver. The app inside it keeps its own visual system, including its own light/dark behavior and color.

Never put a TOHSENO amber overlay over the simulated app. The frame belongs to TOHSENO. The app belongs to its maker.

## 8. "Creativity comes from permission and examples"

A rainbow gradient says that the brand would like to be perceived as creative. It does not make a person more able to create.

TOHSENO creates permission structurally:

- one user is a sufficient reason to make an app;
- the app may remain private forever;
- the idea may be weird, temporary, intimate, local, or commercially useless;
- the maker does not need to call themselves a developer;
- missing is allowed; another Shot is expected.

TOHSENO creates possibility through examples. Show real, sharply specific Shots such as:

- an app for the five people I do not want to lose touch with;
- an app that remembers the stories my daughter invents;
- an app for one ritual nobody else understands;
- an app for a recurring argument in one household;
- an app that exists for a single trip and is then complete.

Specific examples open the mind. Generic claims like "build anything" close it.

The Studio remains restrained so the app icons, simulator, and examples can carry the abundance. **The frame has taste. The work has freedom.**

## 9. Typography

TOHSENO has one typeface of its own and otherwise speaks in the platform's voice.

### 9.1 The Tohseno typeface

TOHSENO's brand typeface is **Tohseno**, a custom Iosevka build compiled from `brand/typeface/private-build-plans.toml`. The typeface is source code: its build plan is a brand token with the same canonical status as `colors.json`, its provenance (Iosevka release tag, commit hash, variant substitutions) is recorded in `brand/typeface/EVOLUTIONS.md`, and the compiled binaries in `brand/typeface/dist/` are build artifacts reproducible from the plan.

Family structure: three weights (Regular, Bold, Heavy) × two widths (Normal, Condensed) × upright only. There is no italic; emphasis is weight. Its glyph decisions carry the protocol: the dotted zero is the target with the arrow in it, and `->` ligates because the contact loop is drawn in arrows.

**The Tohseno typeface has exactly two homes:**

1. **The wordmark and signatures.** The wordmark is set in Tohseno Condensed Heavy; the campaign line in Tohseno Mono Regular, lowercase.
2. **The Factory's terminal voice.** CLI output, build logs, and evidence reports rendered by TOHSENO's own command-line surface. The Factory speaks in its own letterforms at the moment of the press.

It appears nowhere else. Not in Studio UI, not in documentation body text, not in generated apps, not in marketing body copy. Like Chemical Amber, the typeface becomes powerful through scarcity.

### 9.2 Platform typography

Everything that is not the wordmark or the Factory's terminal voice uses the platform's type:

- UI and body: `-apple-system`, SF Pro Text, then Inter as the non-Apple fallback.
- Display declarations: SF Pro Display, then Inter Display.
- IDs, timestamps, states, source lineage, and protocol evidence **in Studio and on the web**: SF Mono, then a neutral monospace fallback. This is deliberate: Studio stays quieter than the Shots it contains. The custom mono belongs to the terminal, not the chrome.

Sentence case is the default for interface copy. Uppercase is reserved for compact labels, IDs, protocol states, and campaign declarations.

### 9.3 The wordmark as paths

The wordmark is distributed as outlined SVG paths so no surface ever depends on the font being installed. The paths are derived from Tohseno Condensed Heavy; if the typeface evolves, the paths are regenerated from the build, never redrawn by hand. The build plan is the source of truth; the SVG is a projection.

## 10. Motion

Motion should reveal consequence, not decorate emptiness.

The signature motion is a single circle drawn when a person commits or graduates something. It should happen once, with slight human variance, and then become still.

Avoid ambient particles, floating cards, pulsing glows, slow parallax, and "AI thinking" theatrics. The system should feel fast and physical.

Respect Reduce Motion. A consequential state change must remain obvious without animation.

## 11. Image language

Show the transition from intention to software:

> sentence → build → real iPhone → use → feedback → evolution → circle

Prefer real screenshots, real devices, contact sheets, source evidence, maker notes, and specific apps.

Do not use robots, glowing brains, generic cosmic gradients, chrome coins, holographic code, or smiling people pointing at laptops.

The cosmic quality belongs in the Void and the scale of possibility, not in visual-effects wallpaper.

### The starfield

The amber-speckled starfield is the Void made visible: sparks of potential before an intention takes form. It is a **campaign asset**, scoped exactly:

Permitted: launch material, movement-level pieces, posters, social, whitepaper covers, slides.
Prohibited: Studio, documentation, provenance surfaces, and all product UI, where the Void is rendered as flat `#050505` and nothing else.

The product is the still Void. The campaign is the Void with its sparks showing.

## 12. Design is a first-class citizen

Design is not a pass applied after the system works. It is part of what **works** means.

This repository treats design decisions as source code:

- Use shared tokens. No arbitrary hex values in product code.
- Reuse canonical logo assets. No redrawn approximations.
- The typeface is compiled from its build plan; no manually edited font binaries.
- Every user-facing pull request includes before/after screenshots.
- Every new feature defines empty, loading, success, failure, and interrupted states.
- Every iPhone interface is checked with Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, keyboard appearance, Reduce Motion, and at least the smallest and largest supported iPhone sizes.
- Every feature must identify the human decision it exists to support.
- Every use of Chemical Amber must identify the human act it represents.
- Every use of the Tohseno typeface must be one of its two homes (§9.1).
- No generated app inherits TOHSENO styling by default.
- No visual complexity is added without removing an equal or greater amount of cognitive complexity.

### Required design review note for user-facing changes

Copy this into the pull request:

```md
## Design impact

Human decision supported:

What becomes orange, and why:

Any use of the Tohseno typeface, and which home it belongs to:

Empty / loading / success / failure / interrupted states:

Smallest and largest iPhone checked:

Dynamic Type / VoiceOver / Reduce Motion:

Screenshots:
```

### Five questions before merging

1. Is the next action unmistakable without explanation?
2. Is TOHSENO quieter than the Shot or app it contains?
3. Did we use amber for agency rather than decoration?
4. Does this feel native to the Apple ecosystem without copying Apple advertising?
5. Did we make the system more coherent, or merely add more interface?

## 13. Anti-patterns

Reject work that feels like:

- a generic AI app builder;
- a crypto token launch;
- a colorful no-code playground;
- a spiritual wellness portal;
- a developer terminal costume;
- a clone of Apple marketing;
- a marketplace optimized for trends rather than personal usefulness.

TOHSENO should be unmistakable because of the relationship it proposes, not because it shouts its logo.

## 15. Amendment rule

This document is allowed to evolve, but not casually.

A change to the primitive, canonical promise, Apple boundary, logo family, palette, typeface scope, or meaning of Chemical Amber requires an explicit Brand Genesis amendment in the same pull request.

The brand is not frozen. It is coherent.
