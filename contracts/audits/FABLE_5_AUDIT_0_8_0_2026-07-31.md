## Methodology

Fable performed a line-by-line manual review of all six in-scope Solidity
sources, all eight Foundry test files, the relevant normative protocol,
ADR 0006, the generation definition, EIP-7951 vector fixture, Robinhood
read-only evidence, readiness record, deployment ceremony, and canary runbook.
It formed its conclusions before reading the internal preaudit, then compared
the results.

Commands Fable actually ran were read-only target-commit checks, two source
diffs, a drift survey, and byte-length checks. Foundry and static-analyzer
results in the readiness record were reviewed but not rerun by Fable because
of the CLI permission restriction.

Fable identified **no Critical, High, or Medium code vulnerability**.

### TOH-01 — P-256 mock does not prove the word layout

`ConfigurableP256Precompile.fallback` checks only that calldata is 160 bytes
and its first word is the expected digest. It never examines bytes 32–159. A
hypothetical wrapper using `digest,x,y,r,s` rather than EIP-7951's
`digest,r,s,qx,qy` would therefore pass the mock-backed tests but fail closed
against the real precompile, bricking authorization.

The frozen implementation is correct: `P256Verifier.sol` encodes
`digest,r,s,x,y`, and the pinned positive vector confirms that the final two
words are `qx,qy`. The historical chain preflight also exercised the official
vectors directly against `0x100`, though not through a deployed wrapper.

**Recommendation:** the real production canary must exercise the deployed
wrapper with real P-256 keys before activation. For the next generation, add a
differential test that verifies all five words against the official vectors.

### TOH-02 — A compromised admin defeats recovery

Any active `DEVICE_ADMIN` may immediately change the recovery authority,
cancel a pending recovery even after maturity, and revoke peer devices down to
itself. The three-day delay protects against a malicious recovery authority,
not against compromise of an admin device. This matches ADR 0006 but is the
most important user-facing security consequence of the design.

**Recommendation:** document this threat clearly and consider timelocking
recovery-authority changes in a future generation.

### TOH-03 — Recovery-free key loss is permanent

`LastDevice` and `LastAdminWithoutRecovery` prevent voluntary removal of the
last usable device, but cannot prevent loss of the underlying secure-enclave
key. A one-device account without recovery then loses its Builder identity and
all ability to transfer controlled shots.

**Recommendation:** clients should treat successful `setRecovery` as a
near-mandatory onboarding step before meaningful use.

### Informational findings and dispositions

- TOH-04 agrees with preaudit I-03. Because the account has no execution or
  asset-moving function and TOHSENO actions use EIP-712 domains, the remaining
  risk is signing-client confusion. Keep the signing UI closed over typed
  TOHSENO actions.
- TOH-05 is deliberate neutrality under ADR 0006. Client verification, not
  registry state alone, establishes that a controller is the approved
  BuilderAccount implementation.
- TOH-06 agrees with preaudit I-02. The party capable of returning excessive
  data is the same controller or recovery authority that can already refuse
  authorization. A bounded-copy assembly helper is still appropriate for a
  successor generation.
- TOH-07 is a rendering and liveness hazard, not an authority escalation.
  Clients must not render unsolicited inbound controllership as endorsement.
- TOH-08 relies on the narrow EIP-6780 same-transaction destruction case and
  leaves only the actor's own shot unusable.
- TOH-09 requires explicit UX: `BuilderAccount` is an identity account, not a
  wallet, and users must not send assets to it.
- TOH-10 creates no iteration-based denial of service. The writer pays the
  permanent storage cost.
- TOH-11 is controlled by nonce consumption and client policy. Registration is
  additionally bounded by the 24-hour commitment lifetime.

## Areas independently found correct

Fable found the following properties internally consistent and correctly
implemented:

1. Strict 129-byte versioned P-256 signatures, curve/range/low-s checks,
   exact-size output equal to integer one, and fail-closed absent-precompile
   behavior.
2. Exact ERC-1271 magic-value handling and rejection of malformed return data.
3. Separate device, recovery, per-controller-registration, and per-shot nonce
   domains with EIP-712 chain and verifying-contract separation.
4. Strict 65-byte, low-s, `v` 27/28 secp256k1 recovery signatures and ERC-1271
   contract recovery support.
5. Epoch invalidation and active-device/admin counter transitions.
6. CREATE2 derivation and harmless idempotent front-running for identical init
   code; Aderyn's packed-encoding report is a false positive because the only
   dynamic segment is followed by fixed-width ABI constructor data.
7. Timestamp-preserving commit/reveal idempotence, inclusive age boundaries,
   relayer neutrality, and reveal authentication.
8. Static-call-only external interactions and state writes after validation.
9. Typehash and domain alignment with the normative protocol.
10. The narrow ancestry-free public-witness design from ADR 0006.

## Comparison with the internal preaudit

Fable agreed with preaudit I-01 through I-04 and with the Slither/Aderyn
false-positive dispositions. It found no unjustified dismissal. It considered
the following points missing or understated:

1. The test mock cannot detect P-256 input-layout errors, so the preaudit gave
   the passing mock suite too much evidentiary weight.
2. The admin-compromise/recovery consequence was not stated in the threat
   model.
3. Permanent recovery-free lockout, unsolicited controller transfer, stuck
   assets, permanent commitment storage, and client-chosen unbounded deadlines
   were not called out.

## Residual risks and deployment prerequisites

Before broadcast or activation:

1. Complete the repository's separate human or accepted competitive-review
   gate and disposition every finding.
2. At ceremony time, rerun the full EIP-7951 positive, negative, infinity, and
   exact-gas probe against the explicit Robinhood RPC and a canonical block.
3. Rebuild and byte-compare the frozen artifacts, recheck the deterministic
   deployer runtime, and prove both predicted addresses are still empty.
4. Establish the owner-approved offline release-authority policy and accepted
   repository deployment authority.
5. Complete the real-time three-day production canary. It must exercise actual
   P-256 authorization through the deployed wrapper; that is the operational
   closure for TOH-01.
6. Before public onboarding, ship/document recovery setup, admin-compromise,
   deadline, non-wallet, and unsolicited-controller rendering policy.

## Recommendation

**CONDITIONAL GO.**

The exact frozen 0.8.0 sources have no identified Critical, High, or Medium
vulnerability. Authorization, replay separation, canonicality, commit/reveal,
and fail-closed behavior are carefully constructed. The conditions above are
mandatory: absent the accepted external review, fresh chain checks,
release/deployment authority, and the real three-day production canary, the
repository's current decision remains NO-GO for broadcast or activation.
